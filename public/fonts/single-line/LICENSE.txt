Single-line (stroke) fonts bundled with erzberg
===============================================

Built by `scripts/build-single-line-fonts.js` from

    https://gitlab.com/oskay/svg-fonts
    https://github.com/isdat-type/Relief-SingleLine
    https://github.com/simoncozens/Relief-Pendot
    https://github.com/golanlevin/p5-single-line-font-resources

The first is Evil Mad Scientist Laboratories' collection of SVG fonts for
Hershey Text v3, which carries the Hershey originals together with the "EMS"
faces converted from openly-licensed typefaces. The others are the Relief
SingleLine family. Only the geometry is reformatted here — the strokes are the
ones the designers drew.

THE EMS FONTS
-------------

Created by Evil Mad Scientist Laboratories from typefaces released under the
SIL Open Font License 1.1, and distributed under those terms. Each face's own
metadata block in the upstream SVG names its source typeface and designer —
EMS Readability from Source Sans Pro Light (Paul D. Hunt, Adobe), EMS Osmotron
from Orbitron (Matt McInerney), EMS Allure from Allura (Rob Leuschke), and so
on. See the upstream repository for the full attribution list.

TwinSans is a Hershey Sans variant by Keith Packard.


THE RELIEF SINGLELINE FAMILY
----------------------------

Copyright 2022 The Relief SingleLine Project Authors
(https://github.com/isdat-type/Relief-SingleLine).

Designed by Élisa Garzelli and Noëlie Dayma of the Graphic Design Department of
the institut supérieur des arts et du design de Toulouse (isdaT), in a project
initiated and led by François Chastanet. Licensed under the SIL Open Font
License, Version 1.1; the copyright statement reserves no font name. The full
licence text ships in the upstream repository at OFL.txt.

Unlike the faces above, Relief was drawn as single-line from the start rather
than converted from an outline typeface, and covers 423 glyphs against the
collection's 216 — most of accented Latin.

Relief SingleLine Pendot is Simon Cozens' fork
(https://github.com/simoncozens/Relief-Pendot), under the same licence. It
differs from Relief in its dots: a period, a colon, an i or j tittle and every
accent built on one is a single short stroke a plotter pen can dab, rather than
a small circle the pen has to trace.


ISO 3098
--------

The lettering standard for technical drawings, adopted 1974 — the alphabet the
standard specifies for exactly the kind of drawing this app produces.

Public domain. The letterforms are File:ISO3098.svg on Wikimedia Commons,
released by its author (User:K7) as own work into the public domain, with no
attribution required:

    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ISO3098.svg

The stroke data was extracted from that file and published as free to use by
AZO (https://domisan.sakura.ne.jp/article/cadfont/cadfont.html), and converted
to the SVG 1.1 font format by Golan Levin.


THE PLOTTER FONTS
-----------------

Two of these are ROM data from machines that drew with a real pen, so their
letterforms were shaped by the same constraint this app exports for.

Commodore 1520 — the four-colour ballpoint plotter sold for the Commodore 64.
Recovered from the plotter's ROM by Jim Brain, Gerrit Heitsch, Silver Dream and
Stewart C. Russell (scruss), and released as the "fifteentwenty" font under the
WTFPL: https://github.com/scruss/fifteentwenty

Apple 410 — the Apple 410 Colour Plotter (c.1983), also sold as the Yokogawa
YEW PL-1000. Its glyphs sit on a 16×16 lattice, one byte per character.
Extracted from the firmware ROM by Adam Mayer (@phooky) under the MIT licence:
https://github.com/phooky/Apple-410

DearPlotter — drawn for pen plotters on purpose rather than by necessity, by
Licia He (http://eyesofpanda.com/), commissioned by the Processing Foundation
and the Tezos Foundation. The DearPlotter Font Generator is licensed CC BY-SA
4.0 and the fonts it generates, including this one, under the SIL Open Font
License 1.1.

All three reach here through Golan Levin's SVG 1.1 font conversions in
https://github.com/golanlevin/p5-single-line-font-resources


WHY THESE ARE HERE
------------------

erzberg's other label faces are outline fonts: Space Mono flattened to contours,
so a plotted letter draws both sides of every stem and the pen goes round each
glyph twice. These are single-stroke faces — the skeleton rather than the
outline — which is what a pen plotter is actually built to follow.
